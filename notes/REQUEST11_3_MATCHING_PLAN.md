# Request 11.3 — force-level matching design

Baseline: stage 2 result commit 6217da2; checkpoint 5f5849b. This is an analytic matching audit, not a new stellar-structure or timing-engine experiment. Existing literature and the known frozen parameter dictionary inform the design; no claim of blinded theory discovery is made.

Use the scalar-charge worldline framework of Khalil et al. (2022), arXiv:2206.13233, with Newtonian orbital masses held at leading order. Derive the charge equation, reciprocal pair forces and energy balance before eliminating the charge. Linearize a stable branch and compare its second-order damped transfer with the one-pole benchmark. Give an explicit inertial-error bound. Derive conditions for fixed companion charges, common pulsar-pair readout, linearity and neglect of companion feedback; record failures rather than adding silent assumptions.

Derive a coplanar hierarchical potential drive through first order separately in eccentricity and inner/outer size ratio. Verify the meaning of ascending-node epochs and pericenter phases against the published timing parameterization, rather than assuming the archived auxiliary drive phases are physical. Report any mismatch. Existing columns support only their six prescribed harmonics and common pair coupling; do not claim validation of missing pair-specific columns or higher harmonics.

Symbolic checks must cover reciprocal forces, total energy loss, linear stiffness, transfer/one-pole difference, common-charge factorization, companion-feedback stiffness, and potential harmonic expansion. Save a reproducible check and result JSON. No new physical numerical bound will be selected from this derivation: the unit-drive analysis and auxiliary historical drive are retained with their different scopes.

Success is either a conditional force-level realization with explicit coefficient mapping and approximation domain, or an exact no-go boundary. Actual neutron-star EOS coefficients, damping/mode inertia and complete timing-likelihood validation are not inferred from an illustrative effective action. A first-order relaxation time is not automatically a Compton time; its transfer has no resonance peak.
