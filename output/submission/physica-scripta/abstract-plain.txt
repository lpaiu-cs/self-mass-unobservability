A relaxation pole can be distinct from an instantaneous response yet indistinguishable in a timing measurement. We formulate a procedure that separates three questions: whether a declared comparator can interpolate the response at the measured carriers, whether the difference survives nuisance fitting, and whether the numerical and noise models support inference. For a known drive at K distinct positive frequencies, a real derivative comparator exactly matches a nonzero single pole if and only if its degree is at least 2K-1. Below that boundary, a projected distance determines local amplitude identifiability; its singular-value bounds expose the loss between coefficient-space distinction and measurement precision. Exact controls exhibit surviving, erased and arbitrarily attenuated differences. Positivity and an independently known relaxation-rate gap give a separate comparator test. These results combine classical interpolation and linear estimation in a free-fall response setting. A stored PSR J0337+1715 analysis illustrates the procedure's limits: fourth-degree comparators retain as little as 4.63 x 10^-6 of the reference information in the tested cases, a physical-drive phase condition invalidates our earlier auxiliary mapping, and recomputed timing derivatives change the nuisance geometry substantially. The archived application supports a conditional methodological case study, not a detected relaxation or an empirical strong-equivalence-principle constraint. The accompanying code checks the analytic controls and replays the stored inference without rerunning the timing engine.
