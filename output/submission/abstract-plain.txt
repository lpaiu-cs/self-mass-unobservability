A linear internal state that relaxes on orbital timescales and couples to the drive and the readout adds a pole to a body's response, but a timing measurement samples that response only at a few orbital carriers and only after nuisance fitting. We state what such a measurement can identify. A nonzero single pole can be matched at K positive carrier frequencies by a derivative comparator of degree N with unrestricted real coefficients if and only if N ≥ 2K − 1. A positive reciprocal relaxation spectrum whose rates exceed a gap obeys an inequality that a slow positive pole violates. For a positive reciprocal spectrum, two exact calibrated complex samples test through a moment-variance inequality whether one observable relaxation time suffices, and recover it when equality holds; finite precision cannot uniformly separate arbitrarily close poles, and a finite periodic response record does not determine the transient response.
